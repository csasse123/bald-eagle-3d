# Muscle, tendon and underside revision

Research checked 11 September 2026. This viewer is an educational reconstruction; it is not a dissection, CT segmentation, or calibrated muscle-force simulation.

## Flight muscles

A study including bald eagles describes an **undivided pectoralis**, with converging fibers reaching the humeral pectoral crest. The supracoracoideus lies deeper and its insertion tendon turns through the triosseal region. It also documents coracobrachialis cranialis at the anterior shoulder. These relationships guide the revised broad pectoralis and added cranial shoulder muscle. This is not the specialized deep slow-muscle arrangement reported in some other soaring birds. [Primary eagle study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5015114/)

Pectoralis and supracoracoideus have complementary roles in wing depression and elevation/rotation. Tendon routing matters as much as muscle shape. Published measurements from other birds cannot be treated as bald-eagle force or activation data. [Biewener, *Muscle function in avian flight*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3130450/)

## Legs and toes

The avian nomenclature reference distinguishes superficial, intermediate and deep digital tendon layers. Intermediate flexors are modeled for **digits II and III only**. The long deep flexors reach the ungual flexor tubercles beneath the keratin sheaths. Exact tendon interconnections vary among birds; this viewer does not assert a specimen-specific pattern. [Nomina Anatomica Avium, hindlimb annotations 121–124](https://www.weber.edu/NAA/HindlimbMmAnnotations.html)

Multi-joint tendon transmission allows a proximal muscle to influence several distal joints. Mechanical studies support separating grasping and perching requirements, rather than treating every toe as an independently powered claw. The viewer's grip remains an attachment-driven demonstration, without validated contact, force, or tendon-locking mechanics. [Backus et al., *Mechanical analysis of avian feet*](https://www.eng.yale.edu/grablab/pubs/Backus_RSOS2015.pdf)

## Visible underside

Christian Sasse's supplied **Focus** and **Eagle beauty** photographs guide the longer overlapping underwing coverts, layered axillary region and breast feather relief. Existing feather identities and counts are retained. The photographs constrain visible appearance; they do not reveal every hidden feather root or establish a complete feather census.

Ventral lighting now reveals surface relief. The studio base and its rings have no renderable geometry, including after Reset. The **Hide controls** button clears the overlays and remains available to restore them.

## Inspection

Open the anatomy sidebar, then **Muscles & tendons**. Choose a region, select a named structure, and use **Inspect** to frame its muscle–tendon pair. Above, Below and Side change the inspection direction. Follicular muscle links remain separately selectable in the layer list and are excluded from the default muscle study so they do not obscure larger structures. **Internal organs** is in the same sidebar.

Every previous selectable identity is preserved. New attachment coordinates, fascicle envelopes and tissue dimensions are approximate. Pose-driven shortening is geometric, not evidence of physiological activation or measured force. Histology, vascular microstructure, tendon compliance and specimen-specific origins/insertions require further data.
